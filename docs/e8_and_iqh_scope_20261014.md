# E8, uniform IQH, and the scope of the current classification

Audit date: 2026-10-14. This is a scope and literature audit; it changes no
formula or classification result. The supplied archive remains authoritative
for the implemented cochain model. A physical identification beyond that
archive's stated bounds requires additional arguments.

**What has actually been implemented.**

The state variables contain a Chern-insulator (CI) decoration, localized
integer charge, and a group-cohomological phase. They do not contain an
independent neutral E8 decoration. This is explicit in
[the symmetry and coefficient section](../formulas/01_symmetry.tex) and
[the scope table](../formulas/11_reproducibility.tex). In particular, omitted
E8 obstruction, gauge, and product operations have not been computed and set
to zero.

There are also two distinct physical-normalization statements in
[the 2+1D section](../formulas/03_two_dimensional.tex):

| Calculation | Established status in the supplied material |
|---|---|
| Relative 2+1D sector, with both uniform response integers zero | The charge-conserving lattice construction fixes the relative charge/phase convention. |
| Uniform electronic IQH with nonzero `n0` | The source has an explicit parity comparison; the full product is an explicit consistent convention without a universal match to a specified physical P-move convention. |
| 3+1D CI/charge/phase tower | Calibrated source, product, joint gauges, and AIII/AII/AI checks; a universal terminal parity comparison is still absent. |
| 4+1D CI/charge/phase tower | The stated cochain identities and equivalence-class group law are established; an unrestricted physical-spectrum identification is not. |

For antiunitary `s1`, invariance already forces the uniform integer `n0` to
vanish. The unresolved nonzero-`n0` comparison should therefore not be applied
indiscriminately to cases where `n0=0`. Conversely, success of a relative test
does not settle the full uniform product. The charge-conserving construction
is Wang, Qi, Fang, Cheng, and Gu (2023),
[Exactly solvable lattice models for interacting electronic insulators in two dimensions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15533),
especially its charge-decoration equations and the incoming trivialization
subgroup. It is not a construction of arbitrary chiral uniform layers.

An obstruction polarization identity determines the derivative of a product
correction. It need not determine its closed-cochain part. Exact local tests
of that identity therefore cannot, by themselves, fix the outstanding
physical stacking convention. Reports of the general nonzero-`n0` campaign
should say “classification of the supplied consistent model,” unless a
separate physical comparison is supplied for the particular case.

**The electronic coefficient lattice and the bosonic embedding.**

Use electron charge one and Hall conductivity in units of `e^2/h`. The
invertible 2+1D coefficient with only charge conservation is

\[
 L=\{(\sigma,c):\sigma,c\in\mathbb Z, c-\sigma\in8\mathbb Z\}
   =\mathbb Z\,I\oplus\mathbb Z\,E,
 \qquad I=(1,1),\quad E=(0,8).
\]

Thus the archive's coordinates are `sigma=n`, `c=n+8e`. Barkeshli, Chen,
Hsin, and Manjunath (2022) derive `sigma=c+8k` and the resulting two-integer
classification in the section on `G_f=U(1)^f` and the class-A example of
[Classification of (2+1)D invertible fermionic topological phases with symmetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11039).
Their stacking section also distinguishes proved special cases from general
conjectural stacking rules; it is not a missing universal product proof for
the present package.

A minimal BIQH state of charge-two bosons has `(sigma,c)=(8,0)`: its Hall
conductivity is two in boson-charge units and eight in electron-charge units.
Consequently, in this coefficient lattice,

\[
 B_{2}=8I-E,\qquad E=8I-B_{2}.
\]

This is a relation between physical response generators, not an equality of
the neutral E8 phase with eight electronic IQH layers. A charge-carrying
bosonic realization of `(8,8)` is also different from the neutral `(0,8)`.
For this coefficient problem alone, quotienting by neutral E8 gives
`L/<E>=Z`, whereas quotienting by the image generated by neutral E8 and
charge-two BIQH gives `L/<E,B2>=Z8`. These quotient computations follow
directly from the displayed lattice. They are not classifications with an
additional point group or time reversal.

**Three objects that must be distinguished.**

Let `T` be the group computed by the retained cochain model, `P` the complete
physical invertible-phase group with the specified symmetry, and
`j:B->P` the map obtained by placing compatible bosonic phases into an
electronic system. A proposed realization `i:T->P`, when established, gives
an image `im(i)`; it does not automatically give an embedding. The quotient
that discards bosonic phases is `P/im(j)`. Neither `im(i)` nor this quotient
is defined by deleting a factor from an abstract invariant-factor list.

In particular, `T` already contains bosonic group-cohomological responses.
It should not be called “the classification with all bosonic phases removed.”
An equality of abstract group orders, or a quotient by a named E8 generator
in a comparison paper, does not construct `i` or establish its kernel.

**What adding an E8 coefficient could require.**

In a full decoration model, E8 has an integer cochain `e_(d-2)` in spatial
dimension `d`, with the orientation/antiunitarity sign action. One must
specify its allowed lifts, obstruction classes, lower completions, incoming
gauge images, and products with the existing fields. The response lattice
alone does not determine these operations. If a larger model has additional
incoming images, two retained representatives could become equivalent; if
it has additional completions, a previously obstructed partial decoration
could acquire a completion. A mixed extension can also relate powers of a
new root to old roots. These are structural possibilities, not observations
of an E8-induced change in the current campaign.

There is, however, a useful bounded absence argument in **3+1D**, stronger
than merely checking `H^1(G,Z_s)=0`. The possible incoming contribution from
the missing 2+1D E8 coefficient starts at `H^0(G,Z_s)`. If `s=0`, its integer
generator has an actual symmetric realization: a neutral bosonic E8 phase
on which the entire internal group acts trivially, stacked with a trivial
electronic system. It is independent of the charge extension and of charge
conjugation. That generator is therefore a permanent cycle of the full
AHSS, so its obstruction differentials vanish as classes. If `s` is
nontrivial, `H^0(G,Z_s)=0`, so there is no such incoming class. After
crystalline equivalence, this argument uses the effective internal `s`.

This argument rules out a new 3+1D incoming identification **from that E8
coefficient** in the usual full AHSS. Applying it directly to this code
still requires identification of its lower tower with the relevant full
filtration and control of the other coefficient sectors. It does not prove
a universal coordinate map or mixed stacking formula. In particular,
`H^1=0` alone was an insufficient justification, even when the conclusion
can be recovered by the permanent-cycle argument. No claim that E8
necessarily kills a current 3D root is supported by this audit.

The same argument does not cover all 4+1D incoming sources, where
nonconstant lower-dimensional E8 decorations can enter. Nor does it alone
establish a general direct-product decomposition. A decomposition in a
specific symmetry class must be justified by a construction or a physical
detector, not assumed from the adjective “neutral.”

**Verified 3+1D reference cases.**

Our convention is `TQT^-1=(-1)^rho Q` and antiunitarity is `s=1`; the
action on the charge circle is `rho+s`. With `alpha` the `C2` character:

| Class | Operator symmetry and archive background | Retained model | Full interacting phase group |
|---|---|---|---|
| AIII | Charge-conjugating antiunitary, `rho=s=alpha`, `omega=0`; its square can be changed by a charge-circle section | `Z8` | `Z8 x Z2` |
| AII | Charge-preserving antiunitary, `rho=0,s=alpha,T^2=(-1)^F`, `omega=alpha^2/2` | `Z2 x Z2` | `Z2^3` |
| AI | Charge-preserving antiunitary, `rho=0,s=alpha,T^2=1`, `omega=0` | `0` | `Z2` |

The retained results and complete-root relations are in
[the calibration section](../formulas/09_calibration.tex).
Daniel Freed and Michael Hopkins (2016 preprint; 2021 publication; v6, 2022)
give the full groups as `TP_4` in Corollaries **9.91, 9.93, and 9.95**,
respectively, of
[Reflection positivity and invertible topological phases](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.06527).
The structures are `Pin^c`, `Pin^(tilde c+)`, and `Pin^(tilde c-)`.
Theorem **9.87** supplies the underlying bordism computation. Quoting the
`TP_4` corollaries avoids confusing their perpendicular normal structures
with tangential conventions. Their map from free to interacting phases
has AIII kernel `8Z`; the full theory does not further trivialize that
physical free root. The mathematical field-theory result is applied to
lattice phases under their stated effective-field-theory assumption.

Three physical checks explain why abstract factor counting is inadequate:

* In AIII, Metlitski proves
  `exp(8 pi i eta)=(-1)^(integral w1^4)`. Thus four copies of the physical
  eta root give the neutral bosonic eTmT phase. The additional FFF bosonic
  response is independent: `CP^2` with the specified half flux has eta
  zero but FFF partition function minus one. See
  [Metlitski (2015), Section VI and Eq. (3.45)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.05663).
  The archive calls this equation (74); the presently served PDF numbers
  it (3.45). Hence quotienting by all bosonic phases removes a nonzero
  element already inside the retained `Z8`. Agreement with `4X=eTmT` is
  stronger than an order comparison, but does not evaluate our unsquared
  cochain state on every structured manifold.
* In AI, the bosonic eTmT phase becomes trivial in the presence of charged
  non-Kramers fermions. Wang and Senthil corrected their original
  `Z2^2` claim to `Z2` for this reason. AII retains two independent bosonic
  contributions in addition to its band-insulator root. See
  [Wang and Senthil (2014), including the 2015 erratum and corrected Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1142v3).
  This is an explicit failure of injectivity of a bosonic-to-electronic
  map. It concerns eTmT and does not, by itself, show an incoming E8
  differential.
* For charge-preserving mirror symmetry, Song, Huang, Fu, and Hermele
  (2017) construct an electronic `Z8 x Z2` classification, with an
  independent neutral E8 paramagnet and a bosonic group-cohomology phase
  equal to four copies of the electronic root. Their surface test keeps
  both electric and thermal Hall response, and shows why charge
  conservation protects the E8 distinction. See
  [Topological phases protected by point group symmetry, Section IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08151).
  This establishes a split E8 contribution for that symmetry and those
  generators; it is not a general splitting theorem.

These checks justify neither “all missing neutral sectors decouple” nor
“the observed discrepancies must come from E8.” They give concrete
benchmarks for an extension of the model and specific protected roots
against which new operations must be tested.

**Requirements for a complete E8 implementation.**

The next mathematical input must specify the full coefficient basis,
including the electronic embedding of charge-two BIQH, and the relevant
E8 obstruction, gauge, and mixed product operations in each dimension.
For 3+1D, the permanent-cycle argument above is a useful constraint on
incoming operations. For every new symmetry benchmark, record a map of
physical roots or partition-function characters alongside the final
abstract group. For nonzero uniform IQH, complete the independent physical
product comparison rather than treating local polarization tests as that
comparison. Existing exact cochain certificates remain valid statements
about the model they actually test.
