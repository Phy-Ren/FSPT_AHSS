# From cochain formulas to a stacking group

The local Python package evaluates the calibrated equations on simplices. The
GAP backend performs the global cohomology, lifting and quotient calculations.
Neither component reads the literature benchmark tables.

## Coefficient systems and resolutions

The CI, charge, extension and phase coefficients carry the actions `s1`, `rho1`,
`rho1+s1` and `s1`, respectively. Differential/cohomology caches distinguish these
actions. Finite inputs are checked for associativity, identity, sign characters
and the twisted U(1) extension cocycle equation.

Finite point groups use HAP resolutions transported to the actual symmetry
group. Wallpaper groups use a resolution of the full affine extension by the
rank-two translation lattice. The finite holonomy table supplies only the
background; it does not replace the affine group in cohomology calculations.

Native resolution cochains use exact row vectors. Comparison maps and their
homotopy transfer them to normalized bar cochains. A native primitive needs the
homotopy correction to become a primitive of the actual nonlinear bar source.
Local simplex face values are based at the first vertex of each face; the cup
and differential operations supply coefficient transport between vertices.

## Obstructions and completed roots

For spatial dimension `d`, begin with charge cohomology `H^d(G,Z_rho)` and phase
cohomology `H^(d+1)(G,U(1)_s)`. Apply the supplied charge source to each integral
charge generator. Its class lies in `H^(d+2)(G,U(1)_s)`. The kernel is computed as
an integer lattice, including the original charge relation lattice. Every
kernel generator receives an actual rational phase completion.

For a CI sheet, first solve its integer obstruction in degree `d+1`. Then solve
the terminal source class modulo the image of the charge differential. A
nonzero raw terminal class can be canceled by changing the charge completion;
the backend rejects a sheet only when the permitted adjustment fails.

Finite signed degree-one CI cohomology in the 3+1D point-group campaign is zero
or `Z2`. In 2+1D the optional uniform IQH calculation instead finds the actual
surviving integer sheet multiplicity and a phase completion. It does not append
a free factor before solving the obstruction equations.

## Presentation and incoming identifications

Start with phase generators, completed charge roots and completed CI roots.
Lift every charge cohomology relation by repeated full stacking, remove its
integer charge by a gauge, and retain the residual phase class. Likewise, a
finite-order CI root is stacked to that order and reduced by its complete
sheet/charge gauge before measuring the remaining charge and phase.

Closed charge gauge parameters and closed CI gauge parameters provide incoming
relations. These can mix charge and phase. The full integer presentation is
reduced by Smith normal form, retaining arbitrary torsion orders and free rank.
Integer elimination also extracts the actual filtration subquotients. The final
group is not inferred from the product of its graded layers.

The `relativeUniformIQH0` result is the subgroup obtained by exact elimination of
the CI columns from the full 2+1D presentation. It permits comparison with sources
that fix the uniform IQH index to zero while retaining the full calculation.

## Filling and certificates

For a wallpaper group, the primitive translation basis is constructed from the
full kernel of the holonomy map and checked to generate that kernel. Restriction
of a charge cochain to the oriented fundamental bar cycle
`[tx|ty] - [ty|tx]` gives electrons per primitive translation cell. Every stacking
relation is checked to have zero total filling. The image generated by charge
roots is recorded separately from any CI-generator filling.

This physical normalization must not be equated automatically with a table's
printed `Z` or `2Z`: a source may count decorations on a glide orbit rather than
electrons per primitive cell. The comparison output records that distinction.

Results retain native source and phase vectors, charge adjustments, root
relations, integer gauge parameters and native chain-identity checks. Unit tests
add independent signed bar identities, exact nonabelian GAP/Python transport,
frozen reference vectors and universal polynomial coefficient certificates.
These have distinct scopes: sampled/nonabelian bar checks are not labeled as
exhaustive checks of every possible global cochain.

## Current global scope

Global campaigns cover spatial dimensions two and three. Spatial dimension four
has the supplied local source, stacking and gauge formulas, but a global 4+1D
classification driver is not implemented. The independent neutral E8 sector and
intrinsic higher-response sectors are not part of the current coefficient tower.
Comparisons with a larger physical classification require the documented sector
projection; no missing sector is silently treated as zero.
